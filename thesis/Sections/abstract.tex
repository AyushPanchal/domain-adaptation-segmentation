\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Aerial traffic surveillance requires reliable object segmentation across changing sensing conditions. Electro-optical (EO) imagery provides rich visible appearance cues, but its performance can degrade under illumination changes, low-light conditions, shadows, and weather variation. Infrared (IR) imagery offers complementary thermal information and is useful in visually degraded conditions, but its appearance differs significantly from EO imagery. This creates a cross-domain challenge for deep learning models, especially for segmentation, where the model must predict object masks rather than only bounding boxes.

This dissertation studies EO/IR aerial traffic segmentation using YOLO-based segmentation models. The work presents a controlled experimental analysis of training strategies for improving IR-domain segmentation and combined EO+IR robustness. The experiments include EO-only baselines, full and object-region grayscale transformations, supervised IR training, mixed EO+IR training, balanced EO+IR training, larger YOLO segmentation models, high-resolution training, and mask-aware ensembling. The dataset is organized into EO and IR image-label splits with twelve traffic-related classes, and the primary evaluation metric is mask mAP50-95.

The results show that direct EO-to-IR transfer is weak, confirming a significant modality gap between visible and thermal aerial imagery. EO-only grayscale transformations provide limited improvement and are not sufficient for strong IR segmentation. The largest performance gain is obtained when real IR supervision is introduced during training. Model scaling further improves performance, and increasing the input resolution from 640 to 960 provides the strongest improvement in strict mask quality. The evaluated mask-aware ensemble does not outperform the best high-resolution single model.

The final results identify the YOLO11l-seg model trained jointly on EO+IR data at 960 resolution as the strongest model for primary IR segmentation, achieving an IR mask mAP50-95 of 0.5250. The YOLO11x-seg model trained jointly on EO+IR data at 960 resolution gives the strongest combined EO+IR performance, achieving a combined mask mAP50-95 of 0.4422. These findings indicate that target-domain IR supervision, mixed-modality training, model capacity, and input resolution are key factors for improving EO/IR aerial segmentation performance.
