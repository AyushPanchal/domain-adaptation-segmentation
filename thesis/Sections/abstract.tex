\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Object segmentation is an important technique in visual data analysis that identifies and localizes objects at the mask level, and is therefore useful for traffic monitoring, urban surveillance, emergency response, and intelligent transportation systems. This dissertation highlights the application of segmentation in EO/IR aerial traffic imagery, where objects such as cars, buses, motorcycles, persons, rickshaws, trucks, and vans are captured from elevated viewpoints under visible and thermal sensing conditions. Electro-optical (EO) images provide rich color and texture information, while infrared (IR) images provide thermal cues that are useful in low-light or visually degraded conditions. However, EO and IR images differ significantly in appearance, contrast, texture, and boundary structure, which creates a domain gap for deep learning based segmentation models.

Several YOLO-based segmentation strategies were explored to study this EO-to-IR domain gap and improve IR-domain mask quality. The experimental methodology includes EO-only training, grayscale-domain transformations, supervised IR training, mixed EO+IR training, balanced EO+IR training, larger YOLO model variants, high-resolution training, and mask-aware ensembling. The proposed workflow follows a controlled experimental structure in which each stage changes one major factor, allowing the effect of modality, supervision, model scale, image resolution, and ensembling to be analyzed separately. The experiments are evaluated using IR-only validation and combined EO+IR validation, with mask mAP50-95 used as the primary metric.

The results show that direct EO-to-IR transfer gives weak segmentation performance, confirming that the visible-to-thermal domain gap is significant for aerial mask prediction. Grayscale-domain transformations provide limited improvement and are not sufficient for strong IR segmentation. The largest improvement is obtained when real IR supervision is introduced during training. Larger YOLO models and high-resolution training further improve strict mask quality, while the evaluated mask-aware ensemble does not outperform the best high-resolution single model. The final results identify the YOLO11l-seg model trained jointly on EO+IR data at 960 resolution as the strongest model for primary IR segmentation, achieving an IR mask mAP50-95 of 0.5250. The YOLO11x-seg model trained jointly on EO+IR data at 960 resolution gives the strongest combined EO+IR result, achieving a combined mask mAP50-95 of 0.4422. These findings highlight the importance of IR supervision, mixed-modality training, model capacity, and input resolution for robust EO/IR aerial segmentation.
